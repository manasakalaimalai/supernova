01 · The room

A room that never existed

A room like this has never been built before. Not for small talk. Not just for fun. It was built so female founders could find each other, stay in touch, and get further than they would alone.

Until October 10th, 2026, that room did not exist in India. Getting in front of the right investors could take months. Finding a co-founder who gets it, or an operator who has already solved your exact problem, was mostly luck.

Supernova is the front door to Nova Residency, India's first residency for women founders. We back AI-native women in their 20s for a month of building together, shipping together, and arriving at Demo Day with something real. Before the residency comes Supernova: one day, 100 founders, 20+ investors, and conversations that turn into companies, cheques and relationships that last for years.

This is only Edition 1. Edition 2 is coming soon.

Doors opened at 4 PM. Within minutes the room filled with founders from across India: space tech, carbon capture, consumer apps, health, and more. For many, it was the first time they had stood in a room built specifically for them.

02 · Opening

Opening speech

Manasa, the founder, opened the day by naming why the room had to exist, and what it could unlock for the women standing in it. She welcomed every founder, set the tone, and made clear that this was not a night of small talk. It was a space built for female founders to connect with one another.

Manasa Kalaimalai, Founder & CEO, Nova Residency

03 · Keynote

The keynote

Revathi Raghunath, CEO and Managing Partner at Outerfield and Co-Founder of Garage Labs Technologies, took the keynote. She spoke to a full room about building with clarity when the path is still unclear, and about the kind of leadership that keeps a company moving when the easy answers run out.

04 · Panel

The panel

Angela Honegger (CEO and Consul General, Swissnex in India), Shalini Pal (Principal, NuVentures), PS Pranika (Founder, ShyShark), and Srishty Jain (Founder and CEO, CoLLearn) sat down together. They talked about what it takes to build in India as a woman: the messy early days, the pivots, the moments they almost quit, and the parts of the story that never make it into a polished deck.

07 · Capital

The money arrived

More than twenty investors showed up. Founders sat with people they had been trying to reach for months, sometimes for years. Some of those conversations will turn into term sheets. Others into relationships that last. All of them mattered that night.

08 · Voices

Supernova connected me to people in one night that I've been looking for since I got here. It would have taken me another six months to meet even half these people otherwise.

Founder

I spoke to investors tonight that I've been trying to get in front of for literally months. Some of them I didn't even know how to reach. Being in the same room, actually having real conversations instead of cold emails, that changes everything.

09 · After sundown

Pouring tonight

Two hundred people stayed for the afterparty: the hundred founders, plus a hundred more from the ecosystem. The investors who came stayed for the entire afterparty. Nobody peeled off early. The room kept going.

The people you met that night are people you will stay in touch with. Co-founders found each other. Operators who had already lived through your exact problem told you what worked. The party went on till 1 AM, and everyone had a great time.
